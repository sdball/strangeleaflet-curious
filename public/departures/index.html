How to ride

Get from the From word to the To word by changing one letter at a time. Every board you stop at has to be a real word.

Take as many stops as you like. When you arrive, your ticket counts them. If a shorter route through everyday words exists, you can try the same train again, or ask to see that route. Rarer words are stations too, and now and then one of them makes a shortcut.

The lamp under a flap lights when it reads the destination's letter.

  • Tap the top half of a flap to turn it up a letter, or the bottom half to turn it down. Hold to keep it spinning.
  • On a keyboard, pick a flap with 1 to 4 or ← →, then type the letter you want, or turn it with ↑ and ↓.
  • Depart (Enter) stops at the word on the board. Back (Backspace) undoes a turn or a stop.
  • The board won't tell you whether a word is a station until you depart for it.
  • The Menu (Esc) has a Hint, which turns a flap to a good next stop. Once you've arrived, its Timetable shows a shortest route.

A new train leaves every day. Another train picks one at random, and the page's link takes anyone who opens it to the same journey.

Lewis Carroll published this puzzle in 1879 and called it Doublets: join two words with a chain of words, each one letter different from the last.
Source: Lewis Carroll, Doublets: A Word-Puzzle (London: Macmillan, 1879).

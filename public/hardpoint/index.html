            How to play

            Tap your mech to give it orders. Tap anything else on the map to see what it is and what it does. On a keyboard, the arrow keys move a cursor over the map, Enter picks a hex or opens your orders, Esc backs out, and each order has a letter key shown beside it.

            Every shot makes a sound, built on the spot by your browser. Sound starts on. The Sound button at the top, or V, turns it off and back on. On an iPhone it plays even with the silent switch on, so use the Sound button to keep it quiet. To hear every weapon without a fight, pick Sound Test on the main menu. See how each sound is built, and change it, on the Sound Bench.

            A turn

            • You get a move and two quick actions. Move up to your Speed in hexes. The quick actions are Boost (move again), Skirmish (fire one weapon), Lock On, and Systems (fire smoke, send a repair drone, and so on). Each quick action works once a turn, so to fire twice, spend both on one full action: Barrage (fire two different weapons). The other full action is Stabilize.
            • Overcharge and your core power are free. Overcharge buys an extra quick action for heat. You take it right away, and it can be one you've already used this turn, like a second Skirmish. Your core power works once per battle.
            • Ending your turn early asks first. If you still have movement or actions left, you'll be asked to confirm.
            • Then every hostile takes a turn. Watch the log, or the bar under the map, to see what they did.

            Attacks

            • Attacks roll a d20 against Evasion. You add your pilot's grit. Accuracy and difficulty cancel one for one, and whatever is left becomes d6s: roll them all, keep the highest, and add it for accuracy or subtract it for difficulty.
            • A total of 20 or more is a critical hit. Every damage die rolls twice and keeps the higher.
            • Armor comes off each hit. Reliable weapons deal their Reliable damage even on a miss.
            • Lock On sets up a shot. It costs a quick action and makes no roll. The next attack against that hostile gains +1 accuracy.

            The ground

            • Open ground costs 1 movement per hex and gives no cover.
            • Rough ground costs 2 movement per hex. It gives no cover and doesn't block sight.
            • Scrub costs 1 movement per hex, like open ground. It gives soft cover, +1 difficulty against ranged attacks, to anything standing in it or seen through it.
            • Smoke works like scrub until the end of the next round.
            • Barricades can't be entered, but you can see and shoot over them. A target right behind one, from the shooter's side, has hard cover: +2 difficulty.
            • Walls can't be entered and block sight completely. A target whose wall only half hides it has hard cover.
            • Cover only stops ranged attacks. Melee attacks and arcing mortars ignore it.

            Getting close

            • Moving next to a hostile engages you both. Your movement stops there, and ranged attacks made while engaged take +1 difficulty.
            • Leaving costs a swing. If you start moving while inside a hostile's threat, it gets a free attack first (overwatch). A melee weapon on your mech does the same to them.
            • Knockback shoves. A hit from a weapon with Knockback pushes its target straight away from the attacker, unless something is in the way. If it happens as you start a move, the move is called off, but you keep your movement.

            Damage and heat

            • HP is only the first layer. At 0 HP your mech loses a point of structure and its HP refills. Then you roll a structure check: a d6 for each point lost so far, keeping the lowest. A 5 or 6 leaves you impaired for a turn, 2 to 4 wrecks a weapon or system, and a 1 can stun or destroy you. Lose all four points and the mech is done.
            • Heat is the price of pushing. Overcharge costs 1 heat, then 1d3, 1d6, and 1d6+4 for each later use in the same battle. Some weapons add heat with every shot, like the Arc Lance's 2. Go past your heat cap and you lose a point of stress and roll an overheat check the same way.
            • Stabilize to recover. It's a full action: spend a repair to refill your HP, or vent all your heat.
            • Brace when a hit would cost structure. It's a reaction: you take half damage, and attacks against you take +1 difficulty until the end of your next turn. The cost is that next turn you get only one quick action and can't move, though a Halberd's Steady Footing still lets it move.

            The hostiles

            • Pickets are grunts. Any damage destroys one, even a miss with Reliable damage. They hang back behind cover and plink at you with carbines.
            • Hounds are fast and fragile. They run you down with Speed 5 and are hard to hit, but six damage drops one.
            • Brutes hit hard and slowly. A Brute's maul knocks lighter mechs back a hex. Stepping away from one gives it a free swing, so pick your moment.
            • Lobbers shoot over walls. Their mortars arc over anything, so cover doesn't help against them. A Lobber has to spend a turn reloading after each shot, and it can't fire at a target right next to it.

            Pilots and the hangar

            • Start by picking a pilot. The main menu lists your pilots. Pick one to open their hangar, where New Game gets a briefing on a fresh battlefield and Continue goes back to the battle they have under way. Each pilot keeps their own battle, so switching pilots never loses one. Wren is there from the start, and New Pilot adds more.
            • Each pilot has their own mechs. Wren starts with a stock Halberd, but a new pilot starts with none. Build mechs in their hangar, then pick one in the briefing. The mech editor only offers frames and gear the pilot's license covers.
            • Every battle ends in a debriefing. It says how the fight went, what it did for the pilot's license, and their record so far, then sends you back to the hangar. Look at the field closes it so you can study the final board first.
            • Pilots start with 2 points to spend. Hull gives +2 HP a point and a repair for every 2. Agility gives +1 Evasion a point and +1 Speed for every 2. Systems gives +1 charge a point to every system that uses charges. Engineering gives +1 heat cap a point. At first no stat can take more than 2.
            • The Halberd is the striker. It's balanced, can't be knocked back, and still moves after bracing. Its core power, Hunting Pattern, gives +1 accuracy and +2 damage.
            • The Rampart is the defender. It has 16 HP but moves only 3. Cover takes another point off each ranged hit against it, and its core power, Aegis, puts up a 10-point shield.
            • The Kestrel is the skirmisher. It has Speed 6 and Evasion 11 but only 10 HP and no armor. Hostiles never get overwatch on it, and its core power, Afterburn, gives free Boosts and +3 Evasion.
            • The Lantern is the artillery. It sees 15 hexes and gets a free Lock On every turn. Its core power, Flare Net, locks on to every hostile in sight and lets your attacks ignore cover.
            • Mounts take gear of their size. A main mount takes a main weapon, a heavy mount takes a heavy or a main weapon, and a system mount takes a system. Everything together has to fit the frame's weight capacity, and each system fits only once.
            • Export to keep a copy. Back Up and Share, on the main menu, saves your pilots and their mechs to a file you can import later, on another device, or send to a friend. Importing adds to what you have and replaces nothing.
            • Reset to start over. Reset, at the end of Back Up and Share, puts back Wren and the stock Halberd and removes everything else.
